Say: Before an advisor pitches a company to a buyer, they must answer three basic questions. Today that means opening the company register and each website by hand. An outdated name or a company that was already sold makes the advisor look unprepared.

Sourcing databases already exist. We do not compete with them. We add a check step that shows where every fact came from.

Speaker notes · slide 3

Say: The advisor types one sentence. An AI planner turns it into an industry code and a few search words in Finnish and English. We search the official register, read each company’s website and quote what matches. The advisor ticks the good ones and exports a report with every source link.

Demo tip: click “Open a saved example” first. It loads instantly and does not depend on the network.

Behind the scenes: Python standard library, local server on port 8787, 53 automated tests. The AI only chooses search settings. It never runs commands. Timing from benchmarks/RESULTS.md (7.3 s to 14.7 s per search).

Speaker notes · slide 4

Say: Click any company and you see exactly why it is on the list: a quote from its website, the link, and the official register record. Here the tool caught something useful. The website says the company merged, so it shows a “Check ownership” warning before anyone pitches it.

We are honest about what public data cannot tell you: revenue, ownership and whether the owners want to sell. The tool leaves these blank on purpose. That is the advisor’s job, and where Mergero adds value.
